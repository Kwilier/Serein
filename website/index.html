Serein

Smoke forecasts that know your child.

Serein forecasts wildfire smoke for every neighborhood up to 3 days ahead, then turns it into a personal action plan for each child with asthma, in their family's language.

The problem

Wildfire smoke is now behind many of the worst air days in the U.S., and children with asthma are hit hardest. 4.8 million U.S. children have asthma (CDC, 2023). Today's forecasts are too coarse, ignore what hundreds of air monitors are measuring right now, and give every family the same generic advice.

Too coarse

Official AQI forecasts cover a whole metro area. Smoke doesn't.

Blind to live sensors

The forecast families read doesn't use what air monitors measured this morning. Serein does, starting with about 400 EPA monitors, with low-cost PurpleAir sensors next.

One size fits all

"Unhealthy for sensitive groups" doesn't tell a parent what their child can handle.

How Serein works

1 · SmokeCast

A graph neural network treats air sensors as a network. Each sensor learns from its neighbors, deciding how much to trust each one, and it can forecast neighborhoods that have no sensor at all. It corrects NOAA's official forecasts using live readings and reports an honest uncertainty range.

2 · AirPlan

A personal Bayesian model learns each child's own smoke sensitivity from a 10-second daily check-in, starting from what medical studies say about all children. It sends a green / yellow / red plan. It can make advice stricter than public guidance, never looser.

3 · Proof

We wrote the test rules down in advance. The final test runs once on 2024-25 fire seasons the model never trained or was tuned on, against NOAA and EPA on the very same days. The numbers shown here are 2023 development results.

A plan made for one child

After a few weeks of check-ins, AirPlan knows whether a child reacts at 20 µg/m³ or is fine at 50. In tests with 300 simulated children, personal plans beat one-size-fits-all advice within about two weeks of logging.

YELLOW for Maya tomorrow: smoke may cause symptoms.
Keep the quick-relief inhaler with Maya, including at school. Swap running and sports practice for indoor activities. Keep windows closed; run an air purifier if you have one.
