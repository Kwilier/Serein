# Interview guide (task E-1)

Goal: 5+ parents of children with asthma, 2+ school nurses, 1+ pediatric clinician,
by Dec 5 so findings land in Brief Q3 (Opportunity) and Q5 (Validation) before the
final edit. 15 minutes each, phone or video. Two people per call when possible: one
asks, one takes notes.

## Rules (from docs/ETHICS.md and Conrad rules)

- No names in notes. Use P1, P2 (parents), N1, N2 (nurses), C1 (clinician). Record
  only role, state, and child's age bracket (e.g., "parent, Sacramento area, child 6-9").
- Ask permission before quoting them, even anonymously. Write "OK to quote: yes/no."
- Do not ask for any medical record or diagnosis detail beyond what they volunteer.
- Do not record audio. Type notes during the call, save as
  `docs/interviews/P1_YYYY-MM-DD.md`. Never commit anything with a real name.
- Interview notes go in the brief as counts and short quotes:
  "Of 6 parents, 4 said X" and one or two quotes, with the person's permission.
- Do not pitch for the first 10 minutes. Learn first, show the plan last.

## Finding people

- Parents: your own school's nurse can forward a two-line ask to families (they
  cannot give you names). Local asthma Facebook groups, AAFA support communities,
  your pediatrician's office bulletin board, youth sports coaches (practice
  cancellations on smoke days), after-school programs.
- School nurses: email the district nurse coordinator (title varies: "Lead Nurse,"
  "Health Services Coordinator"). Your own school's nurse first.
- Clinician: your own pediatrician, a parent on the team who works in healthcare,
  a university pediatric pulmonology division's general inbox, a respiratory
  therapist, a certified asthma educator (AE-C). The clinician review request in
  `docs/drafts/clinician_review_request.md` is separate from this interview.

### Outreach text (adapt; 3 sentences max)

> Hi [Name], I'm a high school student on a team building a free tool that gives
> families of kids with asthma a neighborhood-level wildfire smoke forecast a day
> or two ahead, with a plan for their child. We are entering it in the Conrad
> Challenge (a national student innovation competition) and want to make sure we
> are solving a real problem. Could I ask you 8 to 10 questions for 15 minutes
> this week or next, by phone or video? No names will be recorded.

## A. Parents of a child with asthma (15 min)

**Warm-up (2 min)**
1. How old is your child, and roughly how long have they had asthma?
2. In a normal week, how do you decide whether they can play outside or do sports?

**Smoke days (6 min)**
3. Think about the last bad smoke day you remember. How did you first find out the air was bad? (App, news, smell, school message, someone told you?)
4. When did you find out: the night before, that morning, or after your child was already outside?
5. What did you do differently that day? What did you wish you had done earlier?
6. Did your child have symptoms during or after that smoke? When: same day, next day, two days later?
7. Have you ever had to take them to urgent care, the ER, or call the doctor because of smoke? (Only if they bring it up; do not press.)

**What they use now (3 min)**
8. Which apps or sites do you check for air quality, if any? What's missing from them?
9. Does your child's doctor-provided asthma action plan say anything about smoke or air quality? Do you use it on smoke days?

**Reaction to Serein (3 min, show the message mockup from docs/mockups/messages.md)**
10. If you got this text the evening before a smoke day, what would you do with it? What's confusing or missing?
11. Would you answer a 6-tap check-in each evening (symptoms, inhaler use) for the first few weeks so the plan learns your child? What would make you stop?
12. What language would you want this in? Who else in the family would need it?

**Close**
13. Is there anything we should have asked? May we quote you anonymously?

## B. School nurses / district health coordinators (15 min)

1. On a smoke day, who in the district decides about recess, PE, sports practice, and closures? What information do they use, and when does it arrive?
2. How many students with asthma are you responsible for? How do you track them on smoke days?
3. What did you do during the worst smoke event you've handled? What broke down?
4. Do you use the AirNow Flag Program, PurpleAir, AirNow, the county air district, or anything else? What do you trust most, and why?
5. How far ahead would a forecast need to be to change a decision (e.g., cancel a field trip, move PE indoors)? Is 24 hours enough? 48?
6. Would a forecast for the school's neighborhood rather than the whole metro area change anything for you?
7. If families of students with asthma got a plan for their child the evening before a smoke day, what would you want it to say, and what should it never say?
8. Who would approve a tool like this in your district? Is there a budget line it could come from (safety, health services, communications)? What do you pay for ParentSquare/SchoolMessenger-type tools, if you know?
9. Would you be willing to pilot a free version for one fire season? What would you need from us?
10. May we quote you anonymously? Would you consider writing a short letter of support (we have a template)?

---

## C. Pediatrician / pulmonologist / respiratory therapist / asthma educator (15 min)

1. In your practice, what do you see in the days after a smoke event? How many days after exposure do visits peak?
2. Do your asthma action plans address air quality or smoke? What do you tell families to do on smoke days?
3. Do you believe children differ meaningfully in smoke sensitivity? What signals would tell you a particular child is sensitive?
4. We estimate a child's sensitivity from a daily caregiver check-in (symptoms, rescue inhaler use, activity). What are the pitfalls? What would make the estimate wrong or dangerous?
5. Our plans can only be stricter than EPA guidance, never looser, and always tell families to follow their clinician's action plan and list emergency signs. Is that the right floor? What else is non-negotiable?
6. Show the green/yellow/red messages (docs/mockups/messages.md). What would you change? What is missing?
7. Would a health system or Medicaid plan pay for this? Who would you talk to there?
8. Would you review the final wording and the medical priors table before we submit (20 minutes, written feedback)? May we name you as a reviewer?

---

## Turning interviews into brief text

- Q3 Opportunity: "Of N parents, K first learned about smoke from ___; M found out after the child was already outside." Plus one quote.
- Q5 Validation: "Of N nurses, K said a 24-hour neighborhood forecast would change a recess or PE decision." Plus the one change we made because of what we heard (e.g., added "move practice indoors" to the yellow plan).
- Q8/Q9 Go-to-market and Business model: who approves, which budget line, what comparable tools cost, any pilot willingness.
- Never invent a quote or round up a count.
